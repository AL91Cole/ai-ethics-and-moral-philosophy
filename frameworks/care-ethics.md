# Care Ethics and AI

Care ethics focuses on relationships, dependency, vulnerability, context, and the responsibilities people have toward one another. It challenges moral approaches that treat every problem as an abstract choice between isolated individuals.

## Application to AI

Care ethics asks:

- Who is vulnerable in this relationship?
- Who depends on the system or institution?
- Does the design listen to lived experience?
- Is care being offered collaboratively or imposed paternalistically?
- Does the system protect relationships and self-determination?
- Who performs the often invisible labor that makes the technology work?

An AI support tool may be accurate yet uncaring if it responds mechanically to distress, ignores culture or disability, exposes intimate information, or prioritizes engagement over well-being.

## Strengths

Care ethics contributes:

- attention to context instead of one-size-fits-all rules;
- recognition of vulnerability and unequal power;
- relational accountability;
- listening, responsiveness, and empathy;
- concern for people overlooked by aggregate measures;
- respect for care workers and affected communities.

## Risks and Limitations

The language of care can become paternalistic. A company may claim to know what is best while removing choice. A companion may sound warm while exploiting emotional dependency. Care can also become selective, extending compassion only to people considered familiar or deserving.

Care therefore needs rights, privacy, accountability, truthfulness, and justice as boundaries.

## Connection to Unified Love

Unified Love shares care ethics' attention to relationships, vulnerability, humility, and responsiveness. It adds explicit commitments to unconditional human worth, forgiveness, non-judgment, inclusion without division, and empathy without exploitation.

Care is ethical when it supports a person's dignity and agency rather than making them dependent on the caregiver's power.
