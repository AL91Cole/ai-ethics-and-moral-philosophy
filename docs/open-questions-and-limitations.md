# Open Questions and Limitations

**Document type:** Research agenda

An ethical framework becomes dangerous when it treats its own unresolved assumptions as settled truth. The following questions remain open and should guide future writing, community feedback, design experiments, and evaluation.

## 1. Who defines the protected ethical core?

Multi-stakeholder governance is preferable to unilateral control, but it does not eliminate power. Who selects the stakeholders? How are marginalized perspectives protected from majority rule? What threshold changes a core principle? When should international human-rights norms override local preference?

## 2. How stable should the core be?

If it is too easy to revise, powerful actors can weaken safeguards. If it is nearly impossible to revise, historical blind spots can become permanent. The framework needs a defensible amendment process, emergency process, versioning model, and rollback standard.

## 3. How should conflicts among protected principles be resolved?

Privacy may conflict with immediate safety. Transparency may expose security or personal information. Accessibility adaptations may require sensitive data. Fairness definitions may point toward different outcomes. The current framework provides a deliberative process but no universal formula.

## 4. When is consent genuinely free?

People may click "agree" because they need employment, education, healthcare, public benefits, or social participation. More work is needed on refusal, alternatives, collective consent, children's rights, capacity, and institutional power.

## 5. Can functional empathy be measured without rewarding performance theater?

A system may sound compassionate while manipulating behavior or failing vulnerable people. Evaluation needs to test honesty, dependency risk, non-diagnosis, privacy, cultural humility, refusal behavior, and long-term outcomes—not only user satisfaction.

## 6. What is the correct boundary for emergency intervention?

The distinction between venting and immediate danger protects both safety and honest expression, but thresholds can fail in either direction. Any production system needs qualified clinical, legal, privacy, disability, and lived-experience input. It must also document false positives, false negatives, disparate escalation, and remedies.

## 7. Should legal evidence ever be transferred automatically?

Automatic cryptographic preservation can protect integrity. Automatic external disclosure can violate privacy, due process, or professional duties. A proposed attorney-escrow model still needs clear triggers, client identity, confidentiality rules, conflicts management, jurisdiction, retention, notice, and withdrawal procedures.

## 8. How can deception remain strictly defensive?

Labyrinth boundaries prohibit hack-back, punishment, damage, and pursuit outside the protected environment. Research is still needed on inducement, employee monitoring, bystander data, cross-border sessions, law-enforcement access, evidence admissibility, and safety-critical systems.

## 9. How should attribution uncertainty be communicated?

Technical indicators can be spoofed. A confidence score may still be misunderstood as guilt. The project needs tested language, corroboration rules, analyst training, disclosure limits, and correction procedures for innocent people.

## 10. Could future AI acquire moral status?

The present position denies moral agency to current AI, not necessarily every possible future system. The repository needs a multidisciplinary method for assessing consciousness claims, interests, autonomy, moral understanding, responsibility, and incentives to exaggerate or deny machine status.

## 11. How can shared governance work at scale?

Participation can become symbolic, slow, inaccessible, or dominated by professional organizations. Future work should compare citizen assemblies, community review boards, worker governance, public-interest trustees, independent auditors, and technical change-control models.

## 12. What does economic justice require?

Fair access involves more than making an AI service available. AI can change work, wages, education, public services, creative livelihoods, and the distribution of wealth. The framework should examine labor rights, automation decisions, benefit sharing, procurement, and concentration of compute and data.

## 13. What environmental duties should the framework include?

The current work centers people, power, and privacy but does not yet adequately address energy, water, minerals, hardware supply chains, electronic waste, or harms to communities near infrastructure. Environmental justice should become a dedicated research area.

## 14. How should success be measured?

Candidate measures include:

- preventable-harm rates and severity;
- disparate outcomes across affected groups;
- understandable explanations;
- successful appeals and time to correction;
- unnecessary data reduced;
- accessibility task completion;
- emotional-dependency and manipulation indicators;
- incidents detected and repaired;
- stakeholder influence on actual decisions;
- deployments constrained or stopped by governance.

Metrics can themselves hide values. Qualitative evidence, lived experience, and unresolved dissent must remain visible.

## 15. What should remain human?

Even a well-governed system may be inappropriate for decisions involving punishment, care, freedom, identity, grief, education, or democratic participation. Future work should identify contexts where AI may advise, contexts where it may assist under supervision, and contexts where it should not decide at all.

## Current Limitations of This Repository

- It presents a developing personal framework, not a peer-reviewed theory.
- Most design claims have not been validated through production deployment or independent evaluation.
- The source base is still small and should expand across cultures, disciplines, and viewpoints.
- Standards mappings are high-level and do not establish compliance.
- Legal questions vary by jurisdiction and require qualified counsel.
- Technical examples describe intended safeguards, not verified implementations.
- The author's own experiences and values shape the framework and may leave blind spots.

Naming these limits is not a weakness. It is part of making the work accountable and capable of learning.
