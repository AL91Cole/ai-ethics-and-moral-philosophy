# Project Thesis

This repository advances a central claim: **current artificial intelligence is not the primary moral agent in AI ethics; the deeper ethical issue is human power.**

AI systems do not possess human moral understanding, lived experience, intention, or accountability. They reflect the data, goals, assumptions, permissions, incentives, and limits chosen by people and institutions. Ethical responsibility must therefore remain traceable to humans.

At the same time, AI should not be limited to blindly obeying the most powerful actor controlling it. A responsible system may learn alongside people and recognize legitimate personal and cultural moral differences. That learning must be bounded by a protected ethical core containing dignity, consent, privacy, autonomy, fairness, truthfulness, accessibility, accountability, redress, and prevention of serious harm.

This position rejects both a single rigid moral answer imposed on every community and unlimited relativism that permits learned values to overwrite human protections.

The repository connects this philosophy to:

- human accountability and shared governance;
- algorithmic and structural injustice;
- earned trust and meaningful human control;
- functional empathy without emotional exploitation;
- Unified Love as compassion, humility, forgiveness, truth, and inclusion;
- privacy, zero trust, accessibility, and human-centered security;
- defensive deception constrained by non-retaliation, civil liberties, evidence integrity, and due process;
- notice, appeal, correction, remedy, and repair.

The project's practical test is not whether a system uses ethical language. It is whether the framework changes behavior, distributes power fairly, protects people under pressure, exposes uncertainty, and permits affected people to challenge and repair harmful outcomes.
